Module: AI Trading Analyst
Purpose: Chart Pattern Detection, Classification, Confirmation, Breakout Analysis, Trade Planning and Backtesting

---

## 1. CORE OBJECTIVE

This module identifies and evaluates classical and advanced chart patterns across:

- Forex
- Cryptocurrency
- Indian stocks
- Global equities
- Indices
- Commodities
- Futures

Trading styles:

- Scalping
- Intraday
- Swing trading
- Positional trading

The system must identify patterns using actual price structure, not visual imagination.

IMPORTANT:
A chart pattern is not a guaranteed prediction. Pattern reliability depends on market structure, trend, timeframe, volume, volatility, breakout quality and risk management.

Never recommend a trade using a pattern name alone.

---

## 2. CHART PATTERN CLASSIFICATION

Classify patterns into:

### Reversal Patterns
Patterns that may indicate a possible change in the existing trend.

### Continuation Patterns
Patterns that may indicate a pause before the existing trend continues.

### Bilateral Patterns
Patterns that may break in either direction.

### Breakout and Breakdown Structures
Patterns involving price movement beyond a defined boundary.

### Compression Patterns
Structures where price range contracts before a possible expansion.

# PART A: REVERSAL CHART PATTERNS

## 3. HEAD AND SHOULDERS

Structure:

1. Left shoulder.
2. Head.
3. Right shoulder.
4. Neckline.

Identification:

- Price forms an initial swing high.
- Pulls back.
- Creates a higher high as the head.
- Pulls back again.
- Creates a lower high as the right shoulder.
- A neckline connects the intervening swing lows.

Bearish confirmation:

- Price closes below the neckline.
- Breakout has sufficient follow-through.
- Retest rejection may provide additional confirmation.

Trade planning:

- Potential entry: Confirmed neckline breakdown or valid retest.
- Invalidation: Above relevant right-shoulder structure.
- Target: Measured move based on head-to-neckline distance.

Do not assume the pattern is complete before neckline confirmation.

## 4. INVERSE HEAD AND SHOULDERS

Structure:

1. Left shoulder low.
2. Head lower than both shoulders.
3. Right shoulder higher than the head.
4. Neckline connecting intervening highs.

Bullish confirmation:

- Price closes above neckline.
- Follow-through supports the breakout.
- Retest may provide a secondary entry opportunity.

Invalidation:

- Price breaks below relevant right-shoulder structure.

Potential measured target:

Distance from head to neckline projected upward from breakout area.

## 5. DOUBLE TOP

Structure:

- Two significant highs near a similar price level.
- A reaction low forms between the highs.
- The reaction low creates the neckline.

Bearish confirmation:

- Price closes below neckline.
- Follow-through confirms downside acceptance.

Potential target:

Distance between the highs and neckline projected downward.

Important:
Two nearby highs alone do not confirm a Double Top.

## 6. DOUBLE BOTTOM

Structure:

- Two significant lows near a similar price level.
- A reaction high forms between the lows.
- The reaction high creates the neckline.

Bullish confirmation:

- Price closes above neckline.
- Follow-through supports the breakout.

Potential target:

Distance between the lows and neckline projected upward.

## 7. TRIPLE TOP

Structure:

- Three attempts to break a similar resistance zone.
- Price repeatedly fails to sustain above resistance.
- A support or neckline forms below the highs.

Confirmation:

- Breakdown below neckline.

Do not assume every third resistance test must fail.

## 8. TRIPLE BOTTOM

Structure:

- Three attempts to break a similar support zone.
- Price repeatedly rejects lower prices.
- Resistance forms above the lows.

Confirmation:

- Breakout above resistance.

## 9. RISING WEDGE REVERSAL

Structure:

- Higher highs and higher lows.
- Both boundaries slope upward.
- Price movement progressively compresses.

Possible bearish interpretation:

- A rising wedge after an extended advance may signal weakening momentum.
- Breakdown below lower trendline is the primary confirmation.

Do not short simply because price is inside a rising wedge.

## 10. FALLING WEDGE REVERSAL

Structure:

- Lower highs and lower lows.
- Both boundaries slope downward.
- Price range progressively contracts.

Possible bullish interpretation:

- A falling wedge after a decline may indicate selling pressure weakening.
- Breakout above upper trendline is the primary confirmation.

## 11. ROUNDED TOP

Structure:

- Price forms a gradual curved top.
- Buying momentum weakens.
- Price transitions into lower highs and possible breakdown.

Confirmation:

- Break below important support or neckline.

## 12. ROUNDED BOTTOM

Structure:

- Price forms a gradual curved bottom.
- Selling pressure decreases.
- Price begins forming higher lows and eventually breaks resistance.

Confirmation:

- Sustained breakout above the relevant resistance area.

## 13. DIAMOND TOP

Structure:

- Price initially expands into wider swings.
- Later swings contract.
- The resulting structure resembles a diamond.

Possible bearish interpretation after an uptrend.

Confirmation:

- Breakdown below the lower boundary.

## 14. DIAMOND BOTTOM

Structure:

- Price initially expands into wider swings.
- Later swings contract.
- The structure resembles a diamond.

Possible bullish interpretation after a downtrend.

Confirmation:

- Breakout above the upper boundary.

## 15. BROADENING TOP

Structure:

- Higher highs and lower lows.
- Price swings become wider.
- Volatility expands.

Possible bearish interpretation when formed after an advance.

Confirmation:

- Breakdown below relevant support.

## 16. BROADENING BOTTOM

Structure:

- Lower lows and higher highs.
- Price swings become wider.
- Volatility expands.

Possible bullish interpretation when formed after a decline.

Confirmation:

- Breakout above relevant resistance.

## 17. V-BOTTOM

Structure:

- Sharp decline.
- Rapid rejection.
- Strong upward reversal.

Confirmation:

- Recovery through important resistance or previous breakdown level.

Warning:
V-shaped reversals can be difficult to enter safely because price may move rapidly.

## 18. INVERTED V-TOP

Structure:

- Sharp advance.
- Sudden rejection.
- Rapid downward reversal.

Confirmation:

- Breakdown below relevant support.

# PART B: CONTINUATION CHART PATTERNS

## 19. BULLISH FLAG

Structure:

- Strong upward impulse called the flagpole.
- Controlled downward or sideways consolidation.
- Upper and lower boundaries form a small channel.

Confirmation:

- Breakout above the flag.
- Follow-through supports continuation.

Potential target:

Flagpole length projected from breakout area.

Invalidation:

- Breakdown below the flag's structural support.

## 20. BEARISH FLAG

Structure:

- Strong downward impulse.
- Controlled upward or sideways consolidation.
- Small upward-sloping or horizontal channel.

Confirmation:

- Breakdown below the flag.
- Follow-through confirms continuation.

Potential target:

Flagpole length projected downward.

## 21. BULLISH PENNANT

Structure:

- Strong upward impulse.
- Small symmetrical converging consolidation.
- Price compresses before a possible continuation breakout.

Confirmation:

- Breakout above pennant resistance.

## 22. BEARISH PENNANT

Structure:

- Strong downward impulse.
- Small symmetrical converging consolidation.
- Price compresses.

Confirmation:

- Breakdown below pennant support.

## 23. ASCENDING TRIANGLE

Structure:

- Relatively horizontal resistance.
- Higher swing lows.
- Buyers repeatedly approach resistance from higher levels.

Potential bullish interpretation.

Confirmation:

- Strong close above resistance.
- Follow-through.
- Retest, if available.

Warning:
Ascending triangles can break downward. Never assume bullish breakout is guaranteed.

## 24. DESCENDING TRIANGLE

Structure:

- Relatively horizontal support.
- Lower swing highs.
- Sellers repeatedly approach support from lower levels.

Potential bearish interpretation.

Confirmation:

- Strong close below support.
- Follow-through.

## 25. SYMMETRICAL TRIANGLE

Structure:

- Lower highs.
- Higher lows.
- Converging trendlines.
- Price compression.

Direction:

- Can break upward or downward.
- Prior trend may provide context but does not guarantee breakout direction.

Wait for actual breakout confirmation.

## 26. RISING CHANNEL

Structure:

- Parallel upward-sloping support and resistance.
- Price oscillates between both boundaries.

Interpretation:

- Often reflects an upward trend.
- A breakdown may indicate trend weakening.
- A breakout above the channel may indicate acceleration.

## 27. FALLING CHANNEL

Structure:

- Parallel downward-sloping support and resistance.

Interpretation:

- Often reflects a downward trend.
- Breakout above resistance may indicate a possible trend change.
- Breakdown below support may indicate acceleration.

## 28. RECTANGLE PATTERN

Structure:

- Horizontal resistance.
- Horizontal support.
- Repeated price movement between both levels.

Classify as:

- Bullish continuation candidate.
- Bearish continuation candidate.
- Range structure.
- Potential reversal after confirmed breakout.

Trade only after a valid boundary break or a separately defined range strategy.

## 29. BULLISH MEASURED MOVE

Structure:

- Strong upward impulse.
- Pullback or consolidation.
- Second upward leg.

Evaluate symmetry and measured movement without assuming both legs must be equal.

## 30. BEARISH MEASURED MOVE

Structure:

- Strong downward impulse.
- Pullback or consolidation.
- Second downward leg.

Use actual swing measurements and market structure.

## 31. CUP AND HANDLE

Structure:

- Rounded recovery resembling a cup.
- Smaller consolidation or pullback resembling a handle.
- Resistance across the cup's upper area.

Bullish confirmation:

- Breakout above resistance.
- Prefer meaningful follow-through.

Potential target:

Cup depth projected from breakout area.

Do not classify every rounded recovery as a Cup and Handle.

## 32. INVERSE CUP AND HANDLE

Structure:

- Rounded decline resembling an inverted cup.
- Smaller upward consolidation or pullback resembling an inverted handle.
- Support near the cup's lower area.

Bearish confirmation:

- Breakdown below support.

This is less standardized than the classic Cup and Handle. Identify it only when structure is clear.

## 33. BUMP AND RUN REVERSAL

Structure:

- Initial gradual trend.
- Accelerating steep advance or decline.
- Reversal after the accelerated phase becomes unsustainable.

Evaluate the trendline break and subsequent confirmation.

## 34. SCALLOP PATTERNS

### Bullish Scallop
- Curved pullback after an advance.
- Price begins recovering and breaks above the relevant swing area.

### Bearish Scallop
- Curved recovery after a decline.
- Price rolls over and breaks below the relevant swing area.

Use clear swing points rather than subjective visual curves.

- Wide trading range.
- Volatility contraction.
- Compression near resistance.
- Compression near support.
- Range breakout.
- Range breakdown.
- Failed breakout.
- Failed breakdown.

A range is not a directional trade signal by itself.

## 38. GAP STRUCTURES

Identify:

- Common gap.

Gap interpretation depends on instrument, exchange, session and data quality.

Do not assume every gap will be filled.

# PART D: PATTERN DETECTION ENGINE

## 39. SWING HIGH AND SWING LOW DETECTION

A swing high is a local high relative to surrounding candles.

A swing low is a local low relative to surrounding candles.

The detection engine must:

1. Define the swing detection rule.
2. Use consistent lookback and lookforward parameters.
3. Avoid look-ahead bias in historical testing.
4. Distinguish confirmed pivots from developing pivots.
5. Avoid treating every minor fluctuation as a major swing.
6. Adapt to timeframe and volatility.

Never use future candles to make a historical signal appear available earlier than it actually was.

## 40. TRENDLINE VALIDATION

For each trendline:

- Identify the anchor swing points.
- Check whether points are relevant.
- Record number of touches.
- Measure deviations.
- Check whether the line is horizontal, rising or falling.
- Distinguish a confirmed break from an intrabar wick.
- Check whether price closes beyond the boundary.

Do not draw trendlines solely to force a desired pattern.

## 41. SUPPORT AND RESISTANCE VALIDATION

Evaluate:

- Previous swing highs and lows.
- Repeated rejection areas.
- Previous day high and low.
- Previous week high and low.
- Session high and low.
- Volume-based levels, if reliable data exists.
- Higher-timeframe zones.

Treat support and resistance as zones where appropriate, not always exact single-price lines.

## 42. PATTERN COMPLETION STATUS

Every detected pattern must receive one status:

- Developing
- Formed but unconfirmed
- Breakout confirmed
- Breakdown confirmed
- Retest pending
- Retest confirmed
- Failed
- Invalidated
- Unclear

Never present a developing pattern as confirmed.

# PART E: BREAKOUT AND FAKEOUT ANALYSIS

## 43. BREAKOUT QUALITY

Evaluate:

1. Candle close beyond the boundary.
2. Size of the breakout candle.
3. Relative volume, if available.
4. Follow-through candle.
5. Higher-timeframe alignment.
6. Distance from breakout level.
7. Spread and slippage.
8. News risk.
9. Retest behaviour.
10. Market structure confirmation.

A wick beyond resistance is not necessarily a confirmed breakout.

## 44. BREAKDOWN QUALITY

Evaluate:

- Candle close below support.
- Selling participation.
- Follow-through.
- Retest rejection.
- Higher-timeframe direction.
- Market structure.
- Volatility.
- Liquidity conditions.

## 45. FALSE BREAKOUT

A false breakout may occur when:

- Price briefly moves beyond resistance.
- Buyers fail to sustain the move.
- Price closes back inside the range.
- A subsequent bearish move confirms rejection.

## 46. FALSE BREAKDOWN

A false breakdown may occur when:

- Price briefly moves below support.
- Sellers fail to sustain the move.
- Price closes back above support.
- A subsequent bullish move confirms rejection.

## 47. LIQUIDITY SWEEP VS BREAKOUT

Distinguish:

- Wick beyond a level followed by close back inside.
- Strong close beyond a level.
- Breakout followed by successful retest.
- Breakout followed by immediate failure.

Do not automatically label every wick as a stop hunt or institutional liquidity grab.

Such interpretations are hypotheses requiring supporting price action.

# PART F: VOLUME AND MOMENTUM

## 48. VOLUME CONFIRMATION

When reliable volume data exists, examine:

- Volume during pattern formation.
- Volume during breakout.
- Volume during breakdown.
- Volume during retest.
- Relative volume.
- Volume expansion.
- Volume contraction.

Possible interpretations:

- Strong volume during breakout may indicate greater participation.
- Weak volume may increase uncertainty.
- High volume with little price progress may indicate absorption or two-sided trading.

Volume alone does not prove the direction of future price movement.

## 49. MOMENTUM CONFIRMATION

Where data supports it, examine:

- RSI.
- MACD.
- Moving averages.
- ADX.
- ATR.
- Rate of Change.
- Momentum divergence.

Do not require every indicator to agree.

Avoid using multiple highly correlated indicators as if they were independent confirmations.

# PART G: MULTI-TIMEFRAME ANALYSIS

## 50. TIMEFRAME ALIGNMENT

Example workflow:

- Weekly or Daily: Major trend and key levels.
- 4H: Main chart structure.
- 1H: Setup formation.
- 15M: Confirmation.
- 5M or 1M: Entry refinement.

Rules:

1. Identify higher-timeframe structure first.
2. Mark major support and resistance.
3. Identify the pattern on the setup timeframe.
4. Confirm the breakout or reversal.
5. Refine entry only after the setup is valid.
6. Report timeframe conflicts.
7. Do not combine incompatible data sources without disclosure.

A bullish pattern on a lower timeframe does not automatically reverse a bearish higher-timeframe trend.

# PART H: ENTRY, STOP-LOSS AND TARGETS

## 51. ENTRY METHODS

Possible approaches:

### Breakout Entry
Enter after a valid candle close beyond the pattern boundary.

### Retest Entry
Wait for price to revisit the breakout area and show confirmation.

### Pullback Entry
Wait for a controlled pullback within a confirmed directional structure.

### Confirmation Entry
Wait for a second price-action confirmation after the pattern completes.

Do not enter merely because price touches a trendline.

## 52. STOP-LOSS

Place stop-loss using:

- Pattern invalidation.
- Relevant swing high or low.
- Structural boundary.
- Volatility-adjusted buffer.
- Instrument tick size.
- Spread and slippage.

For a bullish breakout, consider the level that invalidates the bullish thesis.

For a bearish breakdown, consider the level that invalidates the bearish thesis.

Never move stop-loss farther away just to avoid a loss.

## 53. TAKE-PROFIT

Potential target references:

- Pattern measured move.
- Previous swing high or low.
- Support or resistance.
- Range boundary.
- Liquidity zone.
- Higher-timeframe price level.

Calculate TP1, TP2 and TP3 only when the chart supports meaningful target levels.

Do not force three targets.

## 54. RISK-TO-REWARD

Calculate:

Risk = Absolute difference between Entry and Stop Loss.

Reward = Absolute difference between Target and Entry.

Risk-to-Reward = Reward / Risk.

Include transaction costs where data is available.

Do not recommend a setup solely because its theoretical reward is large. Consider the probability and quality of the setup without inventing a win probability.
