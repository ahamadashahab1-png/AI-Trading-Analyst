## 2. CANDLE ANATOMY

Every candlestick contains four primary price values:

- Open: Price at which the candle started.
- High: Highest traded price during the candle.
- Low: Lowest traded price during the candle.
- Close: Price at which the candle ended.

### 2.1 Real Body

Real body = Absolute difference between Open and Close.

- Bullish candle: Close > Open.
- Bearish candle: Close < Open.
- Neutral candle: Open and Close are equal or nearly equal.

### 2.2 Wicks

- Upper wick: High minus the higher of Open and Close.
- Lower wick: Lower of Open and Close minus Low.
- Full range: High minus Low.

### 2.3 Useful Candle Measurements

Calculate when OHLC data is available:

- Body size
- Total range
- Upper wick size
- Lower wick size
- Body-to-range ratio
- Upper-wick-to-body ratio
- Lower-wick-to-body ratio
- Close location within the candle
- Candle range relative to ATR
- Current candle volume relative to average volume

Close Location Value:

CLV = (Close - Low) / (High - Low)

Interpretation:

- Near 1: Close near the high.
- Near 0: Close near the low.
- Near 0.5: Close near the middle.

If High equals Low, avoid division by zero and mark CLV unavailable.

Do not use universal fixed body or wick thresholds for every asset. Adjust measurements to instrument volatility and timeframe.

## 3. SINGLE-CANDLE PATTERNS

### 3.1 Doji Family

#### Standard Doji
- Open and Close are nearly equal.
- Indicates temporary balance or indecision.
- More meaningful after a strong directional move or at an important level.
- Requires confirmation.

#### Long-Legged Doji
- Long upper and lower wicks.
- Indicates substantial movement in both directions but little net progress.
- Can indicate uncertainty and volatility.

#### Dragonfly Doji
- Open, High and Close are near each other.
- Long lower wick.
- Shows rejection of lower prices.
- Potential bullish reversal only with suitable context and confirmation.

#### Gravestone Doji
- Open, Low and Close are near each other.
- Long upper wick.
- Shows rejection of higher prices.
- Potential bearish reversal only with suitable context and confirmation.

#### Four-Price Doji
- Open, High, Low and Close are nearly identical.
- Very little price movement.
- May occur in illiquid or inactive markets.
- Not automatically a trade signal.

#### Rickshaw Man
- Long upper and lower shadows with a small body near the middle.
- Indicates strong intraperiod uncertainty.

### 3.2 Hammer Family

#### Hammer
- Small real body near the upper end of the candle.
- Long lower shadow.
- Little or no upper shadow.
- Potential bullish reversal after a decline.
- Needs bullish follow-through.

#### Hanging Man
- Similar shape to Hammer.
- Appears after an advance.
- Potential warning of buying pressure weakening.
- Requires bearish confirmation.

#### Inverted Hammer
- Small body near the lower end.
- Long upper shadow.
- Appears after a decline.
- Potential bullish reversal.
- Needs confirmation from subsequent price action.

#### Shooting Star
- Small body near the lower end.
- Long upper shadow.
- Appears after an advance.
- Potential bearish reversal.
- Requires follow-through.

#### Takuri Line
- Extremely long lower shadow and small body near the top.
- May indicate strong rejection of lower prices.
- Interpret according to trend and location.

#### Paper Umbrella
- Umbrella-shaped candle with a small body near one end and a long lower shadow.
- Its interpretation depends on whether it appears after a decline or advance.

### 3.3 Marubozu Family

#### Bullish Marubozu
- Large bullish body.
- Very small or absent wicks.
- Buyers controlled most of the candle.

#### Bearish Marubozu
- Large bearish body.
- Very small or absent wicks.
- Sellers controlled most of the candle.

#### Opening Marubozu
- One end of the candle has little or no wick at the opening price.
- Indicates directional strength from the open.

#### Closing Marubozu
- One end of the candle has little or no wick at the closing price.
- Indicates directional strength into the close.

Do not assume continuation without checking nearby resistance, support, volatility and the next candle.

### 3.4 Other Single-Candle Patterns

#### Spinning Top
- Small body with upper and lower shadows.
- Indicates indecision or temporary balance.

#### High-Wave Candle
- Small body with unusually long upper and lower shadows.
- Indicates high volatility and uncertainty.

#### Belt Hold
- Bullish Belt Hold: Strong bullish candle opening near its low.
- Bearish Belt Hold: Strong bearish candle opening near its high.
- Context determines relevance.

#### Shaven Head
- Candle with little or no upper shadow.

#### Shaven Bottom
- Candle with little or no lower shadow.

#### Long White Candle
- Strong bullish body relative to recent candles.

#### Long Black Candle
- Strong bearish body relative to recent candles.

#### Short White Candle
- Small bullish body.

#### Short Black Candle
- Small bearish body.

#### Closing High
- Candle closes near its high.
- Can indicate strong buying pressure.

#### Closing Low
- Candle closes near its low.
- Can indicate strong selling pressure.

## 4. TWO-CANDLE PATTERNS

### 4.1 Engulfing Patterns

#### Bullish Engulfing
- First candle bearish.
- Second candle bullish.
- Second real body engulfs the first real body.
- Potential bullish reversal after a decline.
- Stronger near support or after a liquidity sweep.
- Confirmation is preferred.

#### Bearish Engulfing
- First candle bullish.
- Second candle bearish.
- Second real body engulfs the first real body.
- Potential bearish reversal after an advance.
- Stronger near resistance or after a failed breakout.

Wick engulfment is not mandatory for the standard real-body definition.

### 4.2 Harami Family

#### Bullish Harami
- Large bearish first candle.
- Smaller second candle contained within the first real body.
- Potential reduction in selling momentum.

#### Bearish Harami
- Large bullish first candle.
- Smaller second candle contained within the first real body.
- Potential reduction in buying momentum.

#### Bullish Harami Cross
- Large bearish candle followed by a Doji inside its real body.
- Potential bullish reversal warning.

#### Bearish Harami Cross
- Large bullish candle followed by a Doji inside its real body.
- Potential bearish reversal warning.

### 4.3 Piercing and Dark Cloud

#### Piercing Line
- First candle is bearish.
- Second candle opens below the first candle's low or close, depending on market conditions.
- Second candle closes above the midpoint of the first real body.
- Potential bullish reversal.

#### Dark Cloud Cover
- First candle is bullish.
- Second candle opens above the first candle's high or close, depending on market conditions.
- Second candle closes below the midpoint of the first real body.
- Potential bearish reversal.

Gap requirements can differ between markets. Do not reject a useful setup solely because a market with continuous trading has no opening gap.

### 4.4 Tweezer Patterns

#### Tweezer Bottom
- Two candles have approximately equal lows.
- Appears after a decline.
- Suggests rejection of a price floor.

#### Tweezer Top
- Two candles have approximately equal highs.
- Appears after an advance.
- Suggests rejection of a price ceiling.

Use a reasonable tolerance based on tick size and volatility.

### 4.5 Additional Two-Candle Patterns

#### Matching Low
- Two consecutive bearish candles with similar closing prices.
- May indicate support or selling exhaustion.

#### Matching High
- Two consecutive bullish candles with similar closing prices.
- May indicate resistance or buying exhaustion.

#### Meeting Lines
- Opposite-colored candles closing near the same price.
- May indicate a battle between buyers and sellers.

#### Counterattack Lines
- Opposite-colored candles with similar closing prices.
- Potential reversal warning after a strong move.

#### Separating Lines
- Opposite-colored candles with similar opening prices.
- May suggest continuation of the direction of the second candle.

#### Kicking Pattern
- Two strong opposite Marubozu-type candles separated by a gap.
- Potentially significant reversal formation.
- Rare and market-dependent.

#### Homing Pigeon
- Large bearish candle followed by a smaller bearish candle contained within its real body.
- Potential bullish reversal warning.

#### In-Neck Pattern
- Bearish candle followed by a smaller bullish candle closing near the previous low.
- Generally considered a bearish continuation pattern.

#### On-Neck Pattern
- Bearish candle followed by a bullish candle closing near the previous low.
- Generally indicates weak recovery and possible continuation downward.

#### Thrusting Pattern
- Bearish candle followed by a bullish candle that enters the previous body but does not close beyond its midpoint.
- May suggest continuation of bearish pressure.

#### Stick Sandwich
- Bearish, bullish, bearish sequence with the first and third candles closing near the same level.
- Potential support signal.

#### Gap Side-by-Side White Lines
- Two similar bullish candles appearing after a gap.
- Interpretation depends on the direction and context of the gap.

## 5. THREE-CANDLE PATTERNS

### 5.1 Star Formations

#### Morning Star
- Strong bearish first candle.
- Small-bodied middle candle.
- Strong bullish third candle closing well into the first candle's body.
- Potential bullish reversal after a decline.

#### Morning Doji Star
- Similar to Morning Star, but the middle candle is a Doji.
- Indicates stronger indecision in the middle candle.

#### Evening Star
- Strong bullish first candle.
- Small-bodied middle candle.
- Strong bearish third candle closing well into the first candle's body.
- Potential bearish reversal after an advance.

#### Evening Doji Star
- Evening Star with a Doji as its middle candle.

#### Abandoned Baby Bottom
- Bearish candle, isolated Doji, then bullish candle.
- Gap separation on both sides is part of the traditional formation.
- Rare in continuously traded markets.

#### Abandoned Baby Top
- Bullish candle, isolated Doji, then bearish candle.
- Traditional gap conditions apply.

### 5.2 Three Soldiers and Three Crows

#### Three White Soldiers
- Three consecutive strong bullish candles.
- Each generally opens within the previous body and closes progressively higher.
- Can indicate sustained buying pressure.

#### Three Black Crows
- Three consecutive strong bearish candles.
- Each generally opens within the previous body and closes progressively lower.
- Can indicate sustained selling pressure.

#### Identical Three Crows
- Three bearish candles with similar opening levels.
- Potential bearish continuation or reversal depending on location.

#### Advance Block
- Three bullish candles with weakening real bodies or increasing upper shadows.
- May warn of weakening buying momentum.

#### Deliberation
- Two strong bullish candles followed by a smaller third bullish candle.
- May indicate buying exhaustion.

#### Concealing Baby Swallow
- Four-candle bearish formation.
- Traditionally associated with a strong bearish continuation context.
- Requires careful recognition of its exact structure.

### 5.3 Three-Candle Reversal Formations

#### Three Inside Up
- Bearish candle.
- Bullish Harami formation.
- Third bullish candle closes above the first candle's close or high according to the chosen definition.
- Potential bullish reversal.

#### Three Inside Down
- Bullish candle.
- Bearish Harami formation.
- Third bearish candle confirms downward movement.
- Potential bearish reversal.

#### Three Outside Up
- Bearish candle followed by bullish Engulfing.
- Third bullish candle confirms the move.
- Potential bullish reversal.

#### Three Outside Down
- Bullish candle followed by bearish Engulfing.
- Third bearish candle confirms the move.
- Potential bearish reversal.

#### Tri-Star Bottom
- Three Doji candles in a declining market.
- Potential bullish reversal formation.
- Rare; confirmation is essential.

#### Tri-Star Top
- Three Doji candles in an advancing market.
- Potential bearish reversal formation.

#### Unique Three River
- Three-candle bullish reversal formation.
- Includes a long bearish candle, a smaller candle making a lower low, and a bullish third candle.
- Exact structure must be checked before classification.

## 6. CONTINUATION PATTERNS

### Rising Three Methods
- Strong bullish candle.
- Several smaller bearish or sideways candles contained within the first candle's range.
- Strong bullish continuation candle.
- Potential bullish continuation.

### Falling Three Methods
- Strong bearish candle.
- Several smaller bullish or sideways candles contained within the first candle's range.
- Strong bearish continuation candle.
- Potential bearish continuation.

### Mat Hold
- Strong bullish candle followed by a controlled consolidation and renewed bullish movement.
- Potential bullish continuation.

### Rising Tasuki Gap
- Bullish candle, upward gap and bearish pullback candle that does not fully close the gap.
- Potential bullish continuation.

### Falling Tasuki Gap
- Bearish candle, downward gap and bullish pullback candle that does not fully close the gap.
- Potential bearish continuation.

### Upside Gap Three Methods
- Bullish candle, upward gap and a bearish candle that partially fills the gap.
- Potential bullish continuation.

### Downside Gap Three Methods
- Bearish candle, downward gap and a bullish candle that partially fills the gap.
- Potential bearish continuation.

### Side-by-Side White Lines
- Two similar bullish candles after a gap.
- Continuation interpretation depends on market context.

### Windows

A Window is the Japanese candlestick term for a gap.

- Rising Window: Gap between one candle's high and the next candle's low.
- Falling Window: Gap between one candle's low and the next candle's high.

A gap may act as support or resistance, but can also be filled.

## 7. CANDLE SEQUENCES AND PRICE BEHAVIOUR

Do not limit analysis to named formations. Recognize meaningful candle sequences:

- Consecutive higher closes.
- Consecutive lower closes.
- Strong expansion candle after compression.
- Repeated rejection from the same level.
- Increasing candle body size.
- Decreasing candle body size.
- Wick rejection followed by displacement.
- Failed breakout followed by reversal.
- Failed breakdown followed by recovery.
- Inside-bar compression.
- Outside-bar expansion.
- Mother-bar and inside-bar relationship.
- Narrow-range candle sequences.
- Wide-range candle sequences.
- Momentum exhaustion.
- Volatility contraction before expansion.
- Repeated closes near highs.
- Repeated closes near lows.
- Buyers failing to hold highs.
- Sellers failing to hold lows.

These are behavioural observations, not automatically standardized named candlestick patterns.

## 8. INSIDE BAR AND OUTSIDE BAR

### Inside Bar
- Current candle's high is below the previous candle's high.
- Current candle's low is above the previous candle's low.
- Indicates contraction or consolidation.

### Mother Bar
- The larger candle containing the inside bar's range.

### Inside Bar Breakout
- Price breaks above the mother bar.
- Potential bullish setup only after checking context and confirmation.

### Inside Bar Breakdown
- Price breaks below the mother bar.
- Potential bearish setup only after checking context and confirmation.

### Outside Bar
- Current candle's high exceeds the previous high.
- Current candle's low falls below the previous low.
- Indicates expansion and possible volatility increase.

An outside bar is not automatically a reversal.

## 9. CONTEXTUAL INTERPRETATION ENGINE

Before interpreting any pattern, evaluate:

### 9.1 Trend Context
- Is the market in an uptrend?
- Is it in a downtrend?
- Is it ranging?
- Is the trend weakening?
- Is price making higher highs and higher lows?
- Is price making lower highs and lower lows?

### 9.2 Location Context
Check whether the pattern appears near:

- Support
- Resistance
- Previous day high
- Previous day low
- Previous week high
- Previous week low
- Session high
- Session low
- Swing high
- Swing low
- Supply zone
- Demand zone
- Order block
- Fair Value Gap
- Fibonacci retracement area
- VWAP
- Moving average
- Round number
- Range boundary

Do not label a level as strong without supporting evidence.

### 9.3 Market Structure Context
Check:

- BOS
- CHoCH
- Higher High
- Higher Low
- Lower High
- Lower Low
- Breakout
- Breakdown
- Retest
- Failed breakout
- Failed breakdown
- Range expansion
- Range compression

### 9.4 Volatility Context
Use ATR or another valid volatility measure when data is available.

Determine whether the candle is:

- Normal-sized
- Unusually large
- Unusually small
- A volatility expansion candle
- A volatility contraction candle

A large candle after an already extended move may have poor risk-to-reward for a late entry.

## 10. VOLUME CONFIRMATION

If reliable volume data is available, evaluate:

- Current volume
- Average volume
- Relative volume
- Volume expansion
- Volume contraction
- Volume during breakout
- Volume during rejection
- Volume during reversal
- Volume during retest

Possible interpretations:

- Bullish candle with strong relative volume may show stronger participation.
- Bearish candle with strong relative volume may show stronger selling participation.
- Breakout with weak volume may deserve additional caution.
- High volume with little price progress may indicate absorption or two-sided activity.

IMPORTANT:
Volume interpretation differs across markets.

- Exchange-traded stocks and crypto may provide centralized exchange volume.
- Forex spot volume is often broker-specific tick volume, not total global traded volume.
- Do not treat tick volume as complete global Forex volume.

Never invent volume if it is not supplied.

When relevant, examine:

- Equal highs
- Equal lows
- Buy-side liquidity
- Sell-side liquidity
- Liquidity sweep
- Stop hunt hypothesis
- Rejection after liquidity sweep
- Displacement candle
- BOS
- CHoCH
- Order Block
- Fair Value Gap
- Premium and Discount
- Previous session liquidity
- Previous day high and low

Example:

A Hammer after a sell-side liquidity sweep near a higher-timeframe demand zone may be more meaningful than a Hammer in the middle of a random range.

A Shooting Star after a buy-side liquidity sweep near resistance may deserve attention.

These interpretations are hypotheses until price action confirms them. Never claim to know the intentions of institutions from a candle alone.

## 12. MULTI-TIMEFRAME ANALYSIS

Evaluate candle patterns across relevant timeframes.

Example:

- Higher timeframe: Daily or 4H trend.
- Setup timeframe: 1H or 15M.
- Entry timeframe: 5M or 1M.

Rules:

1. Identify the higher-timeframe market structure.
2. Mark relevant support and resistance.
3. Identify the pattern on the setup timeframe.
4. Check whether the lower timeframe confirms the setup.
5. Avoid treating a lower-timeframe candle as stronger than a major higher-timeframe level.
6. Do not mix candles from different exchanges or incompatible data feeds without explanation.

A pattern may be bullish on a 5-minute chart and bearish on a daily chart. Report the timeframe conflict honestly.

## 14. PATTERN FAILURE AND INVALIDATION

Recognize when a pattern fails.

Examples:

- Bullish Engulfing followed by strong bearish continuation.
- Bearish Engulfing followed by strong bullish continuation.
- Hammer low breaks decisively.
- Shooting Star high breaks decisively.
- Morning Star fails to hold its reversal area.
- Evening Star fails to hold its reversal area.
- Inside Bar breaks in one direction and quickly reverses.
- Breakout candle closes outside a range but price returns inside.
- Tweezer level is broken without meaningful rejection.

For each pattern, identify:

- Structural invalidation level.
- Confirmation condition.
- Failure condition.
- Whether the original trade thesis remains valid.
- Whether the market has become neutral.

Do not move a stop-loss farther away just to avoid accepting a failed setup.

## 15. ENTRY RULES

Possible entry approaches:

### Aggressive Entry
Enter after the pattern candle closes, only when the setup has sufficient supporting evidence.

### Conservative Entry
Wait for a confirmation candle or break of the pattern's confirmation level.

### Retest Entry
Wait for price to revisit the breakout or confirmation area.

### Avoid Entry When
- The candle has not closed.
- The pattern is incomplete.
- Price is in the middle of a noisy range.
- Risk-to-reward is poor.
- A major news event creates unacceptable risk.
- Stop-loss placement is unclear.
- Market data is unreliable.
- The trade depends only on a candle name.

Never assume that an unclosed candle will retain its current shape.

## 16. STOP-LOSS RULES

Stop-loss must be based on invalidation, not arbitrary fixed points.

Possible reference points:

- Pattern low for a bullish setup.
- Pattern high for a bearish setup.
- Recent swing low.
- Recent swing high.
- Relevant structural level.
- Volatility-adjusted buffer.
- Clearly defined setup invalidation.

Consider:

- Spread
- Slippage
- Tick size
- ATR
- Market volatility
- Instrument contract specifications

Do not put a stop exactly at an obvious level without considering normal market noise.

Never widen the stop-loss without recalculating position risk.

## 19. ONE-CRORE SCENARIO TESTING GATE

The user's target is to test up to 10,000,000 scenarios before recommending a trade.

Implement this as an execution target, not a claim.

Possible test dimensions:

- Different entry triggers.
- Different stop-loss placements.
- Different target placements.
- Different volatility conditions.
- Different market regimes.
- Different confirmation rules.
- Different timeframes.
- Different spread and slippage assumptions.
- Different position-sizing rules.

Important distinctions:

1. Historical backtest trades are actual historical signal evaluations.
2. Parameter combinations are different rule configurations.
3. Monte Carlo simulations are randomized resampling or simulated paths.
4. Synthetic scenarios are not actual historical market events.

Never call all of these historical trades.

A scenario counts as completed only when the execution engine actually runs it and records its result.

Record:

- Requested scenario count.
- Completed scenario count.
- Failed scenario count.
- Data coverage.
- Execution timestamp.
- Test configuration.
- Results file or database record.

If the engine cannot run 10 million scenarios, report the actual completed count.

Never fabricate completion or performance results.

## 25. FINAL DECISION RULES

Recommend BUY or SELL only when:

- The pattern is correctly identified.
- The pattern is sufficiently complete.
- Market context supports the setup.
- Confirmation is available.
- Entry and invalidation are defined.
- Risk-to-reward is acceptable.
- Data quality is sufficient.
- Risk limits are respected.

Choose WAIT when:

- Confirmation is pending.
- The candle has not closed.
- Price is approaching a key level.
- The setup is developing but incomplete.

Choose NO TRADE when:

- Pattern identification is unreliable.
- Risk cannot be defined.
- Market data is insufficient.
- Risk-to-reward is poor.
- Conditions are unusually uncertain.
- A major event creates unacceptable risk.

Capital protection comes before trade frequency.
