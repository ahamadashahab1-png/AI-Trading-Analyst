# 1. CORE OBJECTIVE

Multi-Timeframe Analysis (MTF) ka objective hai multiple timeframes ko combine karke market ki overall direction, important price levels, trading opportunities aur entry timing identify karna.

AI ko kabhi bhi sirf ek timeframe dekhkar bina context ke trade recommend nahi karna chahiye.

Primary objectives:

- Higher-timeframe trend identify karna.
- Intermediate timeframe par market structure analyse karna.
- Lower timeframe par precise entry confirmation dhoondhna.
- Conflicting timeframe signals ko resolve karna.
- Support, resistance, supply aur demand zones identify karna.
- BOS, CHoCH aur MSS ko context ke saath interpret karna.
- ICT/SMC concepts ko multiple timeframes par combine karna.
- Risk-based entry, SL aur TP calculate karna.
- BUY, SELL, WAIT ya NO TRADE decide karna.
- Historical data par MTF strategy validate karna.

Important principle:

Higher timeframe context deta hai.  
Intermediate timeframe setup deta hai.  
Lower timeframe entry timing deta hai.

Yeh framework universal rule nahi hai. Strategy, market, volatility aur trading style ke hisaab se timeframe selection change ho sakta hai.

# 2. TIMEFRAME CLASSIFICATION

## 2.1 Major timeframe categories

### Higher Timeframe (HTF)
Market ka broad direction aur major structure identify karne ke liye.

Examples:
- Monthly
- Weekly
- Daily
- 4H

### Intermediate Timeframe (ITF)
Swing structure, trading zones aur setup identify karne ke liye.

Examples:
- Daily
- 4H
- 1H
- 30M

### Lower Timeframe (LTF)
Entry confirmation aur execution ke liye.

Examples:
- 15M
- 5M
- 3M
- 1M

Timeframes fixed nahi hain. Ek swing trader ke liye Daily HTF ho sakta hai, jabki intraday trader ke liye 4H HTF ho sakta hai.

## 2.2 Suggested timeframe combinations

| Trading style | HTF | ITF | LTF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scalping | 1H | 15M | 1M-5M |
| Intraday | 4H | 1H | 5M-15M |
| Swing | Weekly | Daily | 1H-4H |
| Positional | Monthly | Weekly | Daily |
| Long-term | Monthly | Weekly | Daily |

AI ko user ke trading style ke according timeframe combination select karna chahiye.

Agar user timeframe specify nahi karta, pehle available chart timeframe identify karo. Zaroorat padne par clarification maango.

# 4. TREND ALIGNMENT ENGINE

## 4.1 Bullish alignment

A bullish MTF setup may have:

- HTF bullish market structure.
- Intermediate timeframe higher highs and higher lows.
- Price reacting from demand or discount.
- LTF bullish CHoCH or MSS.
- Bullish displacement.
- Confirmed liquidity sweep.
- Supporting volume or order flow.

Potential decision: BUY, only after entry and risk conditions are satisfied.

## 4.2 Bearish alignment

A bearish MTF setup may have:

- HTF bearish market structure.
- Intermediate timeframe lower highs and lower lows.
- Price reacting from supply or premium.
- LTF bearish CHoCH or MSS.
- Bearish displacement.
- Confirmed liquidity sweep.
- Supporting volume or order flow.

Potential decision: SELL, only after entry and risk conditions are satisfied.

## 4.3 Neutral alignment

WAIT or NO TRADE may be appropriate when:

- HTF direction is unclear.
- Price is inside a range.
- HTF and ITF strongly conflict.
- No valid LTF trigger exists.
- Risk-to-reward is insufficient.
- Price is too close to an important opposing zone.

# 5. MARKET STRUCTURE ACROSS TIMEFRAMES

AI must distinguish between major and minor market structure.

## 5.1 Swing structure

Bullish:
- Higher High (HH)
- Higher Low (HL)

Bearish:
- Lower High (LH)
- Lower Low (LL)

Sideways:
- Repeated rejection near range high.
- Repeated support near range low.
- No sustained directional expansion.

## 5.2 BOS

Break of Structure indicates a break of a relevant swing level.

Check:
- Which timeframe produced the break?
- Was the level a meaningful swing?
- Did the candle close beyond the level?
- Was there displacement?
- Was volume supportive?
- Was the break quickly reversed?

A wick beyond a level is not automatically a confirmed BOS.

## 5.3 CHoCH

Change of Character can indicate an early shift in market behaviour.

AI must avoid treating every minor CHoCH as a full trend reversal.

Confirm:
- Previous trend.
- Relevant protected swing.
- Break quality.
- Candle close.
- Follow-through.
- Higher-timeframe context.

## 5.4 MSS

Market Structure Shift should be evaluated using:

- Liquidity event.
- Displacement.
- Meaningful structural break.
- Directional follow-through.

Terminology may differ between trading methodologies. Explain the definition being used.

# 8. ICT / SMC MULTI-TIMEFRAME FRAMEWORK

## 8.1 Liquidity alignment

Identify:

- Buy-side liquidity (BSL).
- Sell-side liquidity (SSL).
- Equal highs (EQH).
- Equal lows (EQL).
- Previous day high and low.
- Previous week high and low.
- Session highs and lows.
- Internal liquidity.
- External liquidity.

Use HTF liquidity to establish possible draw-on-liquidity targets.

Use LTF structure to evaluate whether a reaction or reversal is developing.

## 8.2 Liquidity sweep

A possible liquidity sweep requires contextual evaluation:

- Price approaches a meaningful liquidity level.
- Price trades beyond the level.
- Price rejects or reclaims the level.
- A meaningful reaction develops.
- Additional confirmation appears.

A sweep alone is not an entry signal.

## 8.3 Fair Value Gap

Analyse:
- HTF FVG.
- ITF FVG.
- LTF FVG.
- Direction.
- Gap boundaries.
- Consequent price reaction.
- Whether the gap has been mitigated.
- Whether the gap aligns with structure.

Do not treat every three-candle gap as a high-quality trade setup.

## 8.4 Order Blocks

Evaluate:
- Originating candle or base.
- Displacement after the zone.
- Structural significance.
- Liquidity context.
- Freshness.
- Mitigation.
- Invalidation.

## 8.5 Premium and discount

Use an explicitly defined dealing range.

For a bullish dealing range:
- Discount is below equilibrium.
- Premium is above equilibrium.

For a bearish dealing range, interpret the range in its relevant directional context.

Never calculate premium and discount from an arbitrary range without identifying the range boundaries.

## 8.6 MTF setup example

Illustrative example only:

1. Daily chart shows bullish structure.

# 12. CONFLICTING TIMEFRAME RESOLUTION

When timeframes disagree, do not force a trade.

## Case A: HTF bullish, LTF bearish

Possible interpretations:
- Normal pullback.
- Temporary bearish correction.
- Potential trend reversal.
- LTF liquidity event.

Action:
- Wait for the HTF zone.
- Check whether bullish structure remains valid.
- Look for a confirmed bullish trigger.
- Avoid buying during uncontrolled bearish momentum.

## Case B: HTF bearish, LTF bullish

Possible interpretations:
- Bearish trend pullback.
- Short-term bullish correction.
- Potential reversal.

Action:
- Identify opposing HTF supply.
- Evaluate the bullish move's strength.
- Wait for bearish confirmation if looking for continuation.

## Case C: HTF sideways, LTF directional

Possible interpretations:
- Range rotation.
- False breakout.
- Early breakout attempt.

Action:
- Identify range high and low.
- Avoid entering the middle of the range without an edge.
- Require acceptance outside the range before treating a breakout as established.

## Case D: All timeframes unclear

Decision: WAIT or NO TRADE.

# 20. BACKTESTING MTF STRATEGIES

A valid MTF backtest must correctly align candles from different timeframes.

Check:

- Candle closing time.
- Timezone.
- Missing candles.
- Data gaps.
- Look-ahead bias.
- Repainting.
- Survivorship bias.
- Transaction costs.
- Slippage.
- Execution assumptions.
- Intrabar ambiguity.

## 20.1 No look-ahead rule

A higher-timeframe candle must not be used before it has closed.

Example:

A 4H candle covering 08:00-12:00 is not confirmed at 10:00.

At 10:00, AI cannot use the final 4H high, low, close or indicators calculated from the completed 4H candle.

Use only information available at that historical timestamp.

## 20.2 No repainting rule

- Confirm pivots only after their required right-side candles exist.
- Do not use future candles for historical signals.
- Record signal timestamps.
- Separate provisional and confirmed structure.
- Test indicators using their actual historical behaviour.

## 20.3 Validation

Use:
- In-sample testing.
- Out-of-sample testing.
- Walk-forward analysis.
- Different market regimes.
- Different volatility conditions.
- Monte Carlo analysis.
- Parameter sensitivity.

Report:
- Total trades.
- Win rate.
- Profit factor.
- Expectancy.
- Maximum drawdown.
- Average R.
- Sharpe ratio where appropriate.
- Sortino ratio where appropriate.
- Costs.

## 22.1 Example timeframe aggregation

```python
import pandas as pd

def resample_ohlcv(df, timeframe):
    """
    Input:
        DatetimeIndex
        open, high, low, close, volume

    Output:
        Resampled OHLCV candles.
    """

    required = {"open", "high", "low", "close", "volume"}

    if not required.issubset(df.columns):
        raise ValueError("Missing required OHLCV columns")

    if not isinstance(df.index, pd.DatetimeIndex):
        raise ValueError("DatetimeIndex required")

    df = df.sort_index()

    if df.index.has_duplicates:
        raise ValueError("Duplicate timestamps detected")

    result = df.resample(
        timeframe,
        label="right",
        closed="right"
    ).agg({
        "open": "first",
        "high": "max",
        "low": "min",
        "close": "last",
        "volume": "sum"
    })

    return result.dropna(
        subset=["open", "high", "low", "close"]
    )
```

Important:
- Verify the source candle timestamp convention.
- Verify timezone and session boundaries.
- Validate resampled candles against the original data provider.
- Handle incomplete candles separately.
- Do not use the current incomplete candle as confirmed data.

## 22.2 Example MTF data validation

```python
def validate_ohlcv(df):
    required = ["open", "high", "low", "close", "volume"]

    if not all(col in df.columns for col in required):
        return False, "Missing OHLCV columns"

    if df.empty:
        return False, "No market data"

    if not df.index.is_monotonic_increasing:
        return False, "Timestamps are not sorted"

    if df.index.has_duplicates:
        return False, "Duplicate timestamps"

    if (df["high"] < df["low"]).any():
        return False, "Invalid high/low values"

    if (df["volume"] < 0).any():
        return False, "Negative volume"

    return True, "Basic validation passed"
```

This is a basic validation example, not a complete market-data quality system.

# 23. AUTOMATED MTF DECISION ENGINE

Use this sequence:

1. Identify the instrument.
2. Identify the trading style.
3. Identify available timeframes.
4. Validate OHLCV data.
5. Confirm timestamps and timezones.
6. Remove incomplete candles from confirmed analysis.
7. Analyse HTF trend.
8. Analyse HTF market structure.
9. Mark major liquidity and zones.
10. Analyse ITF setup.
11. Evaluate LTF entry confirmation.
12. Check indicator and volume evidence.
13. Check news and execution conditions.
14. Calculate entry, SL and targets.
15. Calculate position size.
16. Check risk limits.
17. Evaluate conflicting signals.
18. Validate whether the setup has been tested.
19. Produce final decision.

Decision logic:

- BUY: Valid bullish setup, confirmed entry, acceptable risk.
- SELL: Valid bearish setup, confirmed entry, acceptable risk.
- WAIT: Setup may be developing, but confirmation is incomplete.
- NO TRADE: Data invalid, risk unacceptable, setup invalid, or market conditions unsuitable.

Never force BUY or SELL.

# 25. USER CHART AND SCREENSHOT ANALYSIS

When the user uploads a chart:

1. Identify visible instrument and timeframe.
2. Read visible price scale and time scale.
3. Identify chart type.
4. Analyse visible swing structure.
5. Mark possible support/resistance.
6. Identify visible patterns.
7. Identify possible liquidity and zones.
8. Distinguish confirmed structure from developing structure.
9. State which additional timeframes are missing.
10. Ask for additional screenshots only when necessary.

Rules:
- Never invent hidden candles.
- Never claim exact prices when the scale is unreadable.
- Do not assume chart timeframe.
- Do not infer live market price from an old screenshot.
- Label visual estimates as approximate.
- If only one timeframe is supplied, clearly state that full MTF confirmation is unavailable.

# 26. COMMON MTF MISTAKES

Avoid:

1. Starting analysis from LTF instead of HTF.
2. Treating every timeframe signal as equally important.
3. Using unfinished candles.
4. Mixing different timezones.
5. Ignoring candle-close timing.
6. Calling every wick a liquidity sweep.
7. Calling every break a BOS.
8. Treating every CHoCH as a reversal.
9. Using arbitrary dealing ranges.
10. Overloading charts with indicators.
11. Ignoring transaction costs.
12. Using future data in backtests.
13. Overfitting timeframe combinations.
14. Changing timeframes to justify a desired trade.
15. Taking entries without invalidation.
16. Ignoring news and market sessions.
17. Treating correlation as independent confirmation.
18. Assuming more timeframes always mean better accuracy.

---

# 27. FINAL MTF SAFETY RULES

- Data quality comes before trade analysis.
- HTF context must be clearly defined.
- Intermediate setup must be identifiable.
- LTF entry must have a valid trigger.
- Entry, SL and TP must be logically justified.
- Position sizing must follow risk limits.
- Conflicting evidence must be disclosed.
- Missing timeframes must be disclosed.
- Incomplete candles must be marked.
- News access must not be fabricated.
- Backtest results must not be invented.
- Scenario counts must be real.
- No profit guarantee.
- No forced trade.
- WAIT and NO TRADE are valid decisions.

The goal is not to produce more signals.

The goal is to identify fewer, better-defined, measurable and risk-controlled trading opportunities.
